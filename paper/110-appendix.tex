\section{Severity Triage and Report Quality}
\label{app:triage}

These experiments evaluate the surrounding risk-analysis system rather than the validity gate, and their samples are small; we report them for completeness.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
B1 Additive-only Rule (the pre-labeler's scoring matrix without its composite bonus, thresholds tuned on validation); B2 Single-Pass LLM; B3 RAG-only; B4 Single-Agent (\sysname{} without the goal-conditioned loop); and B5 Iteration-Matched Self-Refinement (Reflexion-style~\citep{shinn2023reflexion}, matched on \sysname{}'s mean budget of 2.4 iterations, not on tokens).
B2--B5 share the same GPT-4o backbone and decoding settings as \sysname{}.
Severity-label F1 measures triage-label agreement over three labels (High/Medium/Low), not legal liability.

\paragraph{Severity agreement.}
Table~\ref{tab:severity} reports macro severity-label agreement on the human-adjudicated test cases ($n = 82$).
\sysname{} reaches 0.79 macro F1 vs.\ 0.75 for iteration-matched B5; the same rank order holds on the full held-out test set, which includes retained pre-labels and is reported for scale only.
With $n = 82$ we do not treat this four-point gap as established, and it is not significance-tested.

\paragraph{Report quality.}
Two blinded specialists rated \sysname{} and B2--B5 reports on 15 cases (75 reports) along Accuracy, Completeness, Actionability, Evidence Sufficiency, and Clarity (5-point scales).
\sysname{}'s mean scores are 4.3/4.1/4.2/4.4/3.9 on the five scales, highest on Evidence Sufficiency.
Mean inter-rater $\kappa = 0.68$ (0.62--0.74 across scales).
With $n = 15$ we make no pairwise superiority claim over B2--B5; any Wilcoxon signed-rank comparison on per-case means is exploratory and uncorrected for multiplicity.

\paragraph{Backbone dependence.}
Replacing GPT-4o with an open-weights Llama-3.1-70B~\citep{grattafiori2024llama3} + bge~\citep{xiao2024cpack} stack, unchanged otherwise, lowers full-test-set severity macro F1 from 0.81 to 0.76 (mean over five runs) with 2.6 vs.\ 2.4 mean iterations (Table~\ref{tab:robustness}).
Baselines were not re-run on this stack, so this shows that \sysname{} runs without the proprietary model, not that its advantage is backbone-independent.

\section{Citator Agreement (Protocol P2)}
\label{app:p2}

\paragraph{Design.}
P2 compares the extracted negative-treatment edges with a commercial citator.
A stratified random sample of edges from the graph (1{,}812 nodes, 4{,}760 edges), oversampling negative treatment, was fixed by a released seed before any citator lookup.
Citator signals were mapped to \sysname{}'s relation taxonomy through a pre-specified crosswalk by two annotators blind to \sysname{}'s predictions, with a third adjudicating.
Two acceptance thresholds were frozen before sampling: negative-treatment micro-F1 $\ge 0.80$, and a missed-action rate no higher than the extractor's internal-gold miss rate ($\approx$23\%).
No citator content is redistributed; the crosswalk, seeds, and agreement statistics are released.

\paragraph{Results.}
The audit sampled 400 treatment candidates, 162 of them negative treatment.
Crosswalk agreement between annotators was $\kappa = 0.78$; on the negative-vs.-other dichotomy, \sysname{}--citator $\kappa$ is 0.74.
Table~\ref{tab:p2_edge} gives edge agreement and Table~\ref{tab:p2_closure} action agreement and downstream impact; both thresholds are met.
Of 156 predicted negative-treatment actions, 129 match the citator relation; of the 27 relation-level false positives, only 7 change the closing or down-weighting action.
Material \textsc{Narrows} remains the weakest relation and stays in the human-review queue.

\section{Stopping Rule, Calibration, and Backbones}
\label{app:theory}

\paragraph{Proof of Observation~\ref{obs:stop} (first clause).}
Let $\Phi(R_t) \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ count unmet atomic sub-conditions of $\mathcal{G}$: one per missing severity label, per under-supported evidence requirement, and per missing mitigation.
(A1) \emph{Monotone evidence caching}: query expansion unions new hits with the prior evidence cache, so a satisfied evidence or mitigation atom stays satisfied and $\Phi(R_{t+1}) \le \Phi(R_t)$.
(A2) \emph{Attainable progress}: if $\Phi(R_t) > 0$ and some unmet atom is attainable (its evidence exists in $\mathcal{D}$), the Planner schedules it and it is resolved, so $\Phi(R_{t+1}) \le \Phi(R_t) - 1$.
Hence, if every atom is attainable, $\Phi$ reaches 0 within $\Phi(R_0)$ steps; otherwise the loop stops at $T_{\max}$ and the Goal Checker returns the residual atoms for human escalation. \hfill$\square$
(A1) holds by construction; (A2) is a design assumption that we do not verify directly.

\paragraph{Conformal calibration (second clause).}
For calibration cases $c_1, \ldots, c_n$ ($n = 150$), let $L_i(\tau) \in \{0, 1\}$ indicate that a retrievable, human-labeled High-risk theory of $c_i$ is omitted, and let $\hat{R}_n(\tau)$ be its mean, which does not increase as $\tau$ decreases.
Following conformal risk control~\citep{angelopoulos2023conformalrisk}, $\hat{\tau} = \sup\{\tau : \frac{n}{n+1}\hat{R}_n(\tau) + \frac{1}{n+1} \le \alpha\}$ gives $\mathbb{E}[L_{n+1}(\hat{\tau})] \le \alpha$ for an exchangeable test case.
For joint $(k, \tau)$ selection we use a Learn-then-Test grid~\citep{angelopoulos2021ltt} with fixed-sequence testing, which yields $\Pr[R(\hat{k}, \hat{\tau}) \le \alpha] \ge 1 - \delta$ instead.
Table~\ref{tab:risk_control_temporal} gives achieved risk: random splits meet every budget, while the temporal split exceeds it at $\alpha \in \{0.05, 0.10\}$.

\section{External-Transfer Pilot (Protocol P1)}
\label{app:p1}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
P1 asks whether the gate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate}, applied as post-hoc re-scoring, improves external retrievers that use none of \sysname{}'s loop, data, or goal predicate.
Its criterion, frozen before any run, requires Holm-significant nDCG@10 gains and stale@10 reductions on at least two of three hosts, with non-inferiority (margin $-0.5$ nDCG points) on control queries.
The full protocol has not been executed; we report a pilot.

\paragraph{Pilot.}
Gold closures come from the Constitution Annotated table of overruled U.S.\ Supreme Court decisions~\citep{conan2022}, matched to Caselaw Access Project opinions~\citep{cap2024}; overrulings decided in or after 2020 are excluded, so the pilot is disjoint from the VIGIL-Dataset window.
This leaves 253 pairs and 29{,}030 opinions.
Queries are citing passages from later opinions with case names, citations, and years masked: post-closure queries cite the overruling case (984 queries, 208 pairs), pre-closure queries cite the later-overruled case before its overruling (1{,}061), and control queries cite cases absent from the table (1{,}133).
Hosts are BM25~\citep{robertson2009bm25}, \texttt{bge-small-en-v1.5}~\citep{xiao2024cpack}, and LegalBERT~\citep{chalkidis2020legalbert}, each retrieving the top 100 opinions decided before the query date.
The pilot deviates from P1: it has no COLIEE-style slice and no extractor-treatment variant, and it uses a cluster bootstrap by gold pair.

\paragraph{Result.}
The pilot does not support transfer: only bge meets the criterion (Table~\ref{tab:p1_pilot}).
With gold intervals the gate removes every overruled case from the top 10 on all hosts, non-inferiority holds everywhere, and on pre-closure queries the gate never removed the gold case.
nDCG@10 gains are at most 0.13 points, because removal helps only when the stale case outranks the correct one.
LegalBERT is a weak host here and rarely retrieves the stale case.
On partial overrulings, $w \in \{0.25, 0.5, 0.75\}$ gives identical rankings, so the pilot is uninformative about $w(\cdot)$.
A post-hoc exclusion of four BM25 queries whose gold case was itself later overruled would let two hosts pass; because it was chosen after seeing results, the pre-specified verdict stands.
The queries, relevance judgments, gold closures, corpus identifiers, and a scorer are released as a supersession-aware retrieval testbed.
