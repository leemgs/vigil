\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What drives the gains.} The gains come from knowing \emph{which} authority is still operative---the extracted, validated treatment layer---and from applying that knowledge before generation; the gate itself (Eq.~\eqref{eq:tpg_score}) is intentionally simple, and the agents and ingestion schema are supporting infrastructure. Table~\ref{tab:temporal_ablation} isolates the effect of applying the layer, and B5 rules out extra refinement passes, though not extra LLM calls, as the source of the gain. The residual advantage on the distractor-free PLRE subset further suggests that validity intervals improve ranking among still-operative authorities, not only by discarding closed ones.

\textbf{Construct validity and remaining controls.} Against a commercial citator (App.~\ref{app:p2}), negative-treatment micro-F1 is 0.81, with 20.4\% missed actions (closures or down-weights) and a 3.8\% PLRE decision-flip rate. Transfer to external retrieval stacks is not yet established (App.~\ref{app:p1}): a gold-closure pilot removed stale precedent on three hosts but met its pre-fixed criterion on only one. Backbone-matched baselines are likewise missing: the open-weights re-run covers VIGIL alone. These gaps (cross-stack transfer, backbone-matched controls, and the modest human-adjudicated triage margin whose CI includes zero) are the highest-priority follow-on measurements; they position the treatment layer as a practical, inspectable component rather than a complete replacement for commercial citators or a fully general retrieval operator.

\textbf{Implications for RAG in evolving corpora.} The same gating pattern applies whenever later documents can supersede earlier ones (regulations, product documentation, scientific literature). VIGIL demonstrates that a lightweight, citation-anchored validity layer can be extracted from public text and applied at retrieval time, reducing stale evidence without requiring a full temporal knowledge graph. Realizing that broader claim, however, requires the P0/P1 controls above and the domain-transfer experiments listed as P2 in Limitations.
